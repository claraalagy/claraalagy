Pop-up University

Democratic and participatory governance in the social and circular economy

A collective space for reflection in which people with different relationships to an issue come together to frame it, share experiences, knowledge and practices, and circulate what they learn.

About

The Pop-up University is a space for collective reflection and exchange on the role of democracy in the transition toward a just and inclusive circular economy. Starting from the premise that transforming our modes of production, consumption and exchange cannot rely solely on experts and institutions, it examines forms of participation and governance that can involve a diversity of actors in this transition. It focuses particularly on social economy organizations, whose democratic operating principles offer useful perspectives for rethinking the governance of the circular economy. Through the sharing of experiences and discussion of concrete practices, the Pop-up University seeks to understand the contributions, limits and challenges of participatory governance, including in comparison with governance in for-profit firms.

Methods

The Pop-up University was built around a participatory action-research approach, bringing together people from research and the social economy to collectively explore democratic governance in the circular economy.

It took place over three half-days, each organized around a different perspective. The first, the practitioner perspective, focused on sharing field experiences and identifying needs, barriers and levers. The second, the researcher perspective, invited participants to collectively explore responses to the issues raised. The third, the journalist perspective, focused on synthesizing the discussions and identifying concrete avenues for action.

The process relied on several participatory facilitation tools, including de Bono’s Six Thinking Hats and a Café of Possibilities. A research happy hour also enriched the discussions by bringing scientific knowledge into dialogue with field experience.

Contribution

I took part in the various Pop-up University activities and contributed to collective discussions on democratic governance in social and circular economy organizations. During the research happy hour, I presented findings from my master’s research on working conditions in social economy organizations engaged in the circular economy, helping bring scientific knowledge into dialogue with field experience. I also contributed to sharing the lessons from the process by co-authoring the Pop-up University synthesis report.
