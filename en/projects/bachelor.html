Research project

Reducing food waste in a university cafeteria:

A case study at the CROUS Arsonval cafeteria in Grenoble

Working with the university cafeteria to reduce food waste at both organizational and individual levels

About

Completed in 2020 as the capstone research project for my Bachelor’s degree in Industrial Engineering at Grenoble INP, this collective project focused on reducing food waste at the CROUS Arsonval university cafeteria in Grenoble. Conducted in collaboration with the cafeteria, it sought to identify ways to raise students’ awareness of food waste and propose concrete solutions to reduce it at both organizational and individual levels. The project examined user behaviour, waste-management practices, and communication among the cafeteria, educational institutions and student associations.

Methods

Our approach drew on a review of the literature on food waste and existing prevention measures in collective catering. We also conducted participant observations in the cafeteria to identify the causes of waste and possible prevention levers, and invited users to complete a short questionnaire. We then developed several awareness tools adapted to the university context: posters, information leaflets, digital communications, and a week-long prevention program combining participatory activities with waste weighing. We also proposed longer-term improvements, including installing a bread-waste meter and introducing a protocol for characterizing and quantifying food waste. These proposals were assessed through a technical and financial feasibility study.

Results

The project resulted in a food-waste awareness strategy combining communication materials, participatory activities and waste-monitoring tools. We designed several measures, including a poster campaign, a week-long awareness program and a conceptual prototype for a bread-waste meter, together with an estimate of manufacturing costs. We also developed recommendations to improve communication between the university cafeteria and educational institutions and to establish regular monitoring of the quantities of waste produced. The 2020 public-health crisis limited implementation of the proposed actions, so the project primarily resulted in operational proposals that could be adapted for other collective catering facilities.
